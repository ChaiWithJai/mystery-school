# First learner pilot

Status: planned, not run. The demo's agent walkthroughs do not establish
learning, retention, or scalability. Run an initial adult pilot before testing
the proposed younger learner scenarios. The fictional characters are not
participant records.

## Question

Can a person begin with something they care about, use one relevant concept,
make a change, and explain or demonstrate it in a changed case?

## Session

Allow 20 minutes per participant and record time spent helping separately.
Invite participants with different expression and support preferences. Do not
assign fixed learner types based on language, school history, or movement.

1. Ask what they want to make, understand, or try. Let them choose a pathway or
   decline. Record their words only with permission.
2. Let them attempt the interaction before showing guidance. Accept pointing,
   sound, drawing, demonstration, or a facilitator's transcription.
3. Offer the optional concept when requested or when they identify a difficulty.
   Record which help was supplied and when. Do not replace their first attempt.
4. Save the version they choose, including an unchanged version or disagreement.
   Ask them to show what they changed and how it relates to their original goal.
5. Introduce the changed case below before offering its answer. Record the first
   response separately from an assisted response. A correct click alone is not
   an explanation or demonstration of understanding.
6. Invite a real partner to respond in person, or use the clearly labeled staged
   response. Keep the two evidence types separate. Record the participant's
   next question and whether they want to continue.

## Changed cases

| Path | Case | Observable response | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Music | Keep the phrase fixed. Compare 0.2 s and 0.4 s attacks. | The person shows a longer, less steep rise or describes the change they hear, then chooses a setting for their purpose. The linear envelope slope halves. | Musical taste, hearing acuity, or general calculus mastery. |
| Movement | Keep distance and shape fixed. Double duration from 2 s to 4 s. | The person predicts or demonstrates the slower motion and relates it to time. At equal progress, velocity halves and acceleration quarters under this model. | Boxing skill, impact force, or measured athlete motion. |
| Ideas | Compare the inspected passage with the authored alternative in an unfair-rule case. | The person distinguishes text from interpretation and revises or defends an account using a specific reason. | Agreement with the model as correctness, or a unique philosophical answer. |

## Record

Keep a pseudonymous session ID, voluntary goal, chosen response mode, source
anchor, before/after artifact IDs, assistance, first changed-case response,
later response, partner type, next question, and facilitator minutes. Record
uncertainty and unusable data explicitly. Do not copy unrelated personal notes,
private images, or sensitive histories into prompts.

For each explicit model request, retain its job ID, reported input/output token
counts, elapsed time, result or error, and reported cost if available. Missing
cost remains null. Do not sum reasoning tokens into output tokens without a
runtime definition showing they are disjoint. These figures cover the request,
not all engineering work or the full cost of running a school.

## Review

Classify failures by where the person's goal was lost: beginning, manipulation,
help, saving, sharing, return, or changed case. Preserve the original examples
before proposing changes. Report observed counts with their denominators and
separate independent responses from assisted ones. A small pilot can identify
failures; it cannot establish population-level gains or long-term retention.
