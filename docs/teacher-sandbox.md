# A sandbox in the hands of a teacher

A good teacher can change the course of someone’s life. Mystery School aims to give that teacher more ways to reach a student, especially when the usual explanation has not connected or the student needs a deeper challenge.

The teacher remains the human in the loop. They choose the learning goal, listen to the student, select an experience, review what happened, and decide what to try next. Student interests are openings into a subject, not diagnoses or fixed learner types.

## Use an interest to open a question

A teacher notices a student cares about boxing. They invite the student to explore motion, compare attempts, and describe what changes. That experience can motivate a physics question about position, time, or velocity. The instructional goal is understanding physics; camera estimates are limited observations, not measurements of force or assessments of boxing mastery.

A proposed learning sequence is:

1. Ask what the student wants to do and choose one explicit learning goal together.
2. Let the student try an experience before explaining everything.
3. Ask for a prediction and a comparison between two attempts.
4. Connect the observation to a representation, such as position against time.
5. Ask the student to apply the relationship in a different context.
6. Review the explanation and decide the next activity together.

This sequence is a design target. The current mirror, reflection, simulation, and artifact tools implement parts of it; a complete instrumented physics lesson and transfer assessment are not established.

## Keep the teacher responsible for interpretation

AI can propose a bounded experiment change or select an approved cue. The teacher must be able to understand the evidence behind that suggestion and choose whether it serves this student. A chatbot response, completed activity, or saved trace does not demonstrate understanding.

The prototype includes artifact provenance, a local review studio, and explicit proposal controls. It does not yet establish teacher rosters, individual student accounts, a complete lesson builder, or an evaluated teacher intervention workflow. Those are future product work, not submission claims.

## Evaluate the learning relationship

For a teacher-led pilot, record the chosen concept, the student’s prediction, their attempt, their own explanation, the teacher’s intervention, and a transfer question. Separate camera estimates, self-reports, authored guidance, and model outputs. Success requires evidence that the student can reason about the concept beyond the original activity; engagement alone is insufficient.
